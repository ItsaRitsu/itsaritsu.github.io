Ten years sewing, eight years working with customers. Most jobs start the same way: someone tells me what they need, I take their measurements, and I draft or rework the garment until it fits.

I alter sleeves, collars, waistlines, crotches and pant legs. I set invisible zippers and buttonholes. I work on a machine or a serger. Below are six projects with the drafts and design sheets included, not just the finished pieces.

01

REWORKED DENIM JACKET

Upcycle commission

A secondhand jacket the client loved but never wore. I unpicked the yokes and the upper back, cut new panels in embroidered mesh, and set them into the original seam lines. The topstitching still reads as the jacket's own.

Sheer mesh on heavy denim: two fabrics with nothing in common at one seam. It is stabilized and stitched from the denim side, and the raw mesh edge is left frayed on purpose. Sleeves were re-set, fit taken in at the shoulder.

SKILLS DEMONSTRATED
Alterations to an existing garment Panel replacement Seam ripping & rebuilding Setting sleeves Taking in at the shoulder Sheer & unstable fabrics Matching existing topstitching
02

CUSTOM ESTHETICIAN APRONS

2026 to present

Stylists and estheticians stand in these for ten hours, so I start by asking what they carry and where their hands go. Then I measure the wearer, draft the pattern, and build to that spec. Pocket placement, adjustable straps, fabric that survives color and product.

I sketch the waistband length, the gathered skirt width, a 4 × 37 in ruffle, 6 × 9 in patch pockets cut 7 × 10 for seam allowance, and a shaped sleeve ruffle. Every apron also gets one personal detail: lace, appliqué or a patch.

SKILLS DEMONSTRATED
Measuring a client Pattern drafting to measurement Waistband construction Gathering & ruffle hems Patch pockets Pressing & finishing Client consultation to spec
03

BRIDAL VEIL, DESIGN EXPLORATION

Private client

When a client is not sure what she wants, I draw options. Several lengths, feather trim, lettuce hem, layered tulle, silk ruffles. I pin reference photos beside them, and we talk through veil forward, veil back and chest height until one direction wins.

The finished veil is the feather version. Machine hemmed on tulle, trimmed by hand.

SKILLS DEMONSTRATED
Consulting a customer on options Design iteration Hem finishes: lettuce, rolled, machine Tulle, chiffon & silk Feather & trim application
04

CUSTOM PERFORMANCE WEAR

Professional string trio, 2020

A three garment set for a working chamber ensemble. One color blocked design, three individual fits and color variations. The trio reads as a set on stage, but each dress is cut to its own wearer.

I sourced the coordinating knits. Then I built in the shoulder movement needed for bowing, and the ease needed to play cello seated.

SKILLS DEMONSTRATED
Fitting three different bodies One design across multiple sizes Sewing knits Color blocked panel construction Engineering range of motion
05

HARLEQUIN JACKET

Costume commission

Drafted from the wearer's recorded measurements and built as a diamond patchwork. Every point matched by hand before the panels were joined. Gold braid along each seam.

The satin collar is interfaced and set with a clean roll line. The beaded trim on the back yoke went on one drop at a time. Fully lined, hemmed by hand, fitted over two rounds of revisions.

SKILLS DEMONSTRATED
Drafting from recorded measurements Fittings & revisions Precision piecing Collar setting & interfacing Lining Trim & bead application Hand finishing
06

COSTUMES & COSPLAY

2015 to present

Cosplay is where I learned to match a spec exactly. Someone hands me a reference image and expects the garment to look like it. Not close. The same.

It also puts me on fabrics that punish mistakes. Vinyl keeps every needle hole. Satin shows every pucker. Lace has to be placed, not just sewn. These are the pieces I am proudest of.
